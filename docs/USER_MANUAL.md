# Pillar Time User Manual

This is a plain-English guide to Pillar Time.

It is written so a 12 year old can understand it.

You do not need to be a programmer. You do not need to know special productivity words. You only need to know this:

**Pillar Time helps you decide what matters today, put it on your calendar, and stop carrying the whole plan in your head.**

## 1. What Pillar Time Is

Pillar Time is a desktop app for managing your day.

It is not just a calendar.

It is not just a to-do list.

It is not mainly a news app.

It is more like a smart daily command center.

It looks at the pieces of your day and helps you answer:

**What should I do next, and why?**

Pillar Time can use:

- your calendar;
- your tasks;
- your Linear issues;
- your reminders;
- your meeting notes;
- your standing commitments;
- your trusted personal context;
- optional news and research sources;
- optional Telegram and audio delivery.

The main point is not to collect more information.

The main point is to make your day easier to see.

## 2. The Big Idea

The most important thing in Pillar Time is a **commitment**.

A commitment is something you are responsible for.

Examples:

- "Prepare for the 2 PM meeting."
- "Send the client follow-up."
- "Finish the Linear issue."
- "Protect a deep work block."
- "Do the weekly review."
- "Make time for health."

Some commitments come from your calendar.

Some come from Linear.

Some come from meeting notes.

Some come from reminders.

Some come from your own brain.

Pillar Time tries to put these in one place.

That way, you do not have to check five different tools just to understand your day.

## 3. How Pillar Time Reduces Mental Clutter

Mental clutter is the feeling that too many things are floating around in your head.

It sounds like this:

- "I need to remember that."
- "Something is due soon."
- "I forgot to follow up."
- "I should check Linear."
- "I need focus time, but my calendar is full."
- "What was I supposed to do after that meeting?"
- "I know I am missing something."

That is tiring.

Pillar Time reduces mental clutter in five ways.

### 1. It Gives Loose Thoughts A Home

When a thought pops up, put it somewhere.

Do not keep repeating it in your head.

Use:

- Quick Capture;
- Planner;
- Running To-Do;
- Reminders;
- Meetings;
- Trusted Context.

Once the thought is stored, your brain can stop holding it.

### 2. It Separates Real Commitments From Noise

Not everything on your calendar is really yours.

Not every all-day event means you are busy all day.

Not every urgent thing is important.

Pillar Time should help separate:

- real meetings from shared calendar noise;
- hard deadlines from loose ideas;
- important work from busywork;
- your commitments from other people's context;
- useful reminders from annoying reminders.

### 3. It Turns Important Work Into Time

A task list can grow forever.

A calendar has real limits.

If something matters, it needs time.

Pillar Time should help turn important work into calendar blocks.

Example:

Instead of only saying:

> Finish launch notes.

Pillar Time should propose:

> 10:00 AM to 11:30 AM - Deep Work - Finish launch notes.

That makes the plan honest.

### 4. It Shows Tradeoffs

You cannot do everything today.

A good plan should say what gets protected and what gets pushed.

Example:

> Protect the deep work block. Inbox cleanup moves later. That is the right trade because the deep work unblocks the release.

That is useful because it makes the cost clear.

### 5. It Lets You Fix The Plan Without Starting Over

Sometimes Pillar Time will miss something.

That is normal.

You should be able to add the missing fact and regenerate the day.

Example:

> Protect a second 30-minute follow-up block after deep work.

Then Pillar Time should rebuild the plan using that new context.

## 4. The Simple Daily Routine

If you only use one routine, use this one:

1. Open **Today**.
2. Add anything important that is floating around in your head.
3. Click **Generate Day Plan**.
4. Read **Today's Three**.
5. Check the proposed calendar blocks.
6. Add missing context if the plan is wrong.
7. Regenerate if needed.
8. Approve the calendar only if it looks right.
9. Start with the first real block of work.

You do not need to set up everything perfectly.

Start with today.

## 5. First Setup

Start small.

Pillar Time should still be useful before every connector is connected.

The best first setup is:

1. Add your identity statement.
2. Add your standing commitments.
3. Add your running to-do list.
4. Connect Google Calendar.
5. Connect Linear if you use it.
6. Add a model provider if you want better coaching language.
7. Add optional connectors later.

### Identity Statement

Your identity statement tells Pillar Time who it is helping.

It also tells the app how to talk to you.

You can include:

- your name, if you want;
- what kind of work matters most;
- what you are trying to protect;
- what you tend to avoid;
- what kind of tone helps you;
- what the app should challenge you on.

Example:

> Help me spend time on work that matters. Be direct. Tell me when I am overcommitting, avoiding the hard thing, or letting urgent noise crowd out important work.

This helps Pillar Time sound like a useful coach instead of a generic report.

### Standing Commitments

Standing commitments are things that keep mattering over time.

Examples:

- protect deep work most mornings;
- keep family time clear;
- follow up with key people quickly;
- write every weekday;
- make time for health;
- review the week every Friday;
- do not let urgent noise crowd out important building work.

Standing commitments help Pillar Time compare today's schedule to what you said matters.

### Running To-Do List

The running to-do list is where loose tasks go.

It does not need to be neat.

Examples:

- "Email Alex about the contract."
- "Review Linear backlog."
- "Book dentist."
- "Draft proposal."
- "Send meeting follow-up."

The point is to stop using your brain as the storage place.

### Google Calendar

Google Calendar helps Pillar Time understand your real schedule.

The app should use Calendar to know:

- what time is already taken;
- which events are yours;
- which events are shared context;
- which all-day events are only background;
- where there is room for focused work.

Important rules:

- Your primary calendars matter more than shared calendars.
- Normal all-day events should not block the whole day.
- Shared or subscribed calendars should usually be treated as context.
- Declined events should usually be ignored.
- Accepted timed events on your primary calendar are usually hard blocks.

If you want Pillar Time to create approved calendar blocks, Calendar must be connected with write permission.

### Linear

Linear helps Pillar Time understand project work.

When Linear is connected, Pillar Time can:

- show your assigned issues;
- group work by project;
- notice blocked issues;
- notice stale issues;
- notice work due today;
- include Linear work in Today's Three;
- propose calendar blocks for Linear execution.

Linear work should become protected time, not just another list.

### Model Provider

A model provider helps Pillar Time write better coaching language.

Without a model provider, the app should still create a basic plan.

With a model provider, the plan should feel more personal and more useful.

### Optional Connectors

Optional connectors can include:

- Telegram;
- Reddit;
- X;
- RSS feeds;
- YouTube;
- podcasts;
- ElevenLabs audio;
- speech-to-text.

Do not turn on everything just because it exists.

Connect what helps your day.

## 6. Today

Today is the main screen.

Use Today when you want to know what to do now.

Today should show:

- your current day plan;
- Today's Three;
- Highest Leverage Today;
- proposed calendar blocks;
- your calendar timeline;
- reminders;
- approval items;
- a place to add missing context.

### Generate Day Plan

Click **Generate Day Plan** when you want Pillar Time to build or refresh your plan for the day.

The app should:

1. read your preferences;
2. read your identity statement;
3. read your standing commitments;
4. read your running to-do list;
5. read your trusted context;
6. read Calendar;
7. refresh Linear;
8. read reminders, meetings, and tasks;
9. classify calendar events;
10. find conflicts, risks, and prep gaps;
11. rank the most important work;
12. choose Today's Three;
13. propose calendar blocks;
14. create approval items for outside actions;
15. write a coaching-style day brief.

The day plan should not be a news report.

It should be about your time, work, promises, meetings, and next actions.

### Today's Three

Today's Three are the three things Pillar Time thinks are most important to protect today.

They should not just be the three loudest things.

They should be the things that matter most because they:

- unblock other people;
- protect a standing commitment;
- reduce risk;
- move an important project forward;
- prevent future chaos;
- fit the real shape of the day.

If the day gets messy, come back to Today's Three.

Ask:

**If I only finish these, would today still count?**

### Highest Leverage Today

High leverage means a small amount of focused work creates a large result.

Examples:

- one hard email that prevents a week of confusion;
- one focus block that unblocks a release;
- one meeting prep block that changes the quality of the conversation;
- one recovery block that keeps you reliable tomorrow.

Pillar Time should explain:

- why this matters;
- what tension it relieves;
- what it displaces;
- what the next step is.

### Add Context & Regenerate

Sometimes Pillar Time will miss something.

Use **Add Context & Regenerate** when you need to correct or add information.

Examples:

- "Protect a second 30-minute follow-up block after deep work."
- "The all-day school event is not mine."
- "This client call matters more than it looks."
- "Do not schedule anything after 5 PM."
- "I am low energy today. Leave more buffer."
- "The Linear issue is blocked until Max answers."

You add the missing fact.

Pillar Time tries again.

### View Brief

Use **View Brief** when you want the fuller explanation behind the day plan.

The brief should explain:

- what matters today;
- why it matters;
- what tension it relieves;
- what it displaces;
- what needs approval;
- what might go wrong;
- what question you may be avoiding.

It should feel like a clear coach talking plainly.

It should not feel like a stiff report.

## 7. The Day Plan

The day plan is the main output of Pillar Time.

It should answer:

- What is already on my calendar?
- What work matters most?
- What needs prep?
- What needs follow-up?
- What needs approval?
- What should I protect?
- What can wait?
- What am I avoiding?

A good day plan should have these parts.

### Executive Read

This is the short summary.

It should tell you the truth about the day.

Example:

> Today is meeting-heavy. The important move is to protect one deep work block before noon and keep follow-up contained so it does not eat the afternoon.

### Today's Calendar And Prep

This shows your meetings and important schedule facts.

It should point out:

- hard blocks;
- soft context;
- all-day events;
- prep needs;
- follow-up needs;
- real conflicts.

### Today's Three

This is the short list of protected commitments.

These are the things Pillar Time thinks deserve the most protection today.

### Highest Leverage Today

This is a longer list of useful actions.

Each item should explain why it matters.

### Linear Focus

This shows Linear work that should affect your day.

It should not just list every issue.

It should highlight the work that is due, blocked, stale, or high leverage.

### Commitments And Follow-Ups

This catches promises and loose ends.

This is where Pillar Time should help prevent dropped balls.

### Approval Queue

This shows actions Pillar Time wants permission to take.

For example:

- add calendar blocks;
- create a Linear issue;
- add a Linear comment;
- update a Linear issue.

### Schedule Protection

This tells you what time needs to be defended.

Example:

> The 10:00 AM deep work block should be protected. Moving it will likely push the release work into the evening.

### Missing Info / Watchouts

This tells you what the app is unsure about.

Example:

> Calendar ownership is unclear for the all-day event. It was treated as background context, not a hard block.

## 8. Calendar Planning

Calendar planning is one of the most important parts of Pillar Time.

Pillar Time should not treat every calendar item the same.

### Hard Blocks

A hard block is time that is truly busy.

Examples:

- a meeting you accepted;
- a call you need to attend;
- a doctor appointment;
- a flight;
- an event that really uses your time.

Pillar Time should not schedule over hard blocks.

### Soft Context

Soft context is useful information that may not block your time.

Examples:

- a shared calendar event;
- a team milestone;
- a holiday;
- a subscribed calendar item;
- an all-day reminder.

Pillar Time should notice soft context, but it should not treat it like a real meeting unless there is a good reason.

### Background Context

Background context is even lighter than soft context.

Examples:

- birthdays;
- public holidays;
- other people's school calendars;
- team calendars you only watch.

Background context can be useful, but it should not take control of your day.

### Ignored Events

Some events should be ignored for planning.

Examples:

- declined meetings;
- old imported events;
- duplicate events;
- shared events that clearly are not yours.

### All-Day Events

Most all-day events should not block the whole day.

Examples:

- holidays;
- birthdays;
- shared team events;
- school calendar notes;
- travel reminders from another calendar.

Some all-day events may matter, like a real travel day or an offsite.

But the app should be careful.

It should not assume all-day means busy all day.

### Primary Calendars And Shared Calendars

Your primary calendars are the calendars that represent your real schedule.

Shared calendars are often only context.

Pillar Time should know the difference.

If it gets this wrong, add context and regenerate.

Example:

> Only my primary calendars should be treated as mine. Shared calendars are context unless I accepted the event.

### Proposed Calendar Tiles

After generation, Pillar Time may show proposed calendar blocks across the top of Today.

Examples:

- 9:00 AM - Deep Work;
- 10:30 AM - Follow-up;
- 1:00 PM - Linear Execution;
- 3:30 PM - Meeting Prep;
- 4:30 PM - Admin.

These blocks should fit around real hard calendar events.

They should not be placed over accepted meetings.

They should not treat normal all-day events as blocking the whole day.

### Approve Calendar

If the proposed schedule looks right, click **Approve Calendar**.

Pillar Time should then create the approved blocks on your Google Calendar.

Generating a plan does not write to your calendar.

Approving the calendar gives permission.

If approval fails, check Settings. Your Calendar connection may need write permission.

## 9. Linear Work

Linear is where many project tasks live.

The Linear screen lets you review project work from inside Pillar Time.

When Linear is connected, you can:

- choose a team;
- filter issues;
- group issues by project;
- see assigned work;
- create issues;
- update issues;
- add comments.

Pillar Time should use Linear when it builds the day plan.

It should notice:

- issues due today;
- blocked issues;
- stale issues;
- issues that unblock other people;
- high-priority issues that need focus time.

One important job of Pillar Time is to make time for Linear work.

If a Linear issue is important, Pillar Time can propose a calendar block for it.

Example:

> 10:00 AM to 11:30 AM - Linear Execution - Finish calendar approval bug.

That way, project work becomes real time on the calendar.

### Linear Changes

Pillar Time can help change Linear, but it should be careful.

It may let you:

- create an issue;
- move an issue to a different state;
- add a comment;
- update details.

Generated agent actions should need approval.

Manual actions you take inside the Linear screen may happen directly, but the app should still be clear about what changed.

## 10. Planner, Commitments, And To-Dos

### Planner

Planner is for work that needs a place but is not always on your calendar yet.

Use it for:

- tasks;
- important dates;
- follow-ups;
- work you may want to schedule later.

Planner should feed the Today page.

If something is important in Planner, Pillar Time should consider it when it ranks the day.

### Commitments

A commitment is something you are responsible for.

It might have a due date.

It might have a person waiting on it.

It might have no date but still matter a lot.

Pillar Time should help track:

- title;
- notes;
- status;
- owner;
- due date;
- priority;
- blocker;
- waiting-on person;
- related project;
- related meeting;
- source system;
- evidence;
- confidence.

You should be able to see what is active, what is blocked, what is done, and what needs attention.

### Standing Commitments

Standing commitments are the responsibilities that keep mattering even when they are not urgent.

Examples:

- family time;
- health;
- writing;
- deep work;
- key client relationships;
- team leadership;
- weekly review;
- learning;
- finances.

If your calendar is full of urgent things but none of them match your standing commitments, Pillar Time should say so.

### Running To-Do List

The running to-do list is a place to dump tasks and loose thoughts.

Use it when something pops into your head and you do not want to lose it.

You can clean it up later.

The point is to move the thought out of your brain and into the system.

### Quick Capture

Use Quick Capture when a thought appears and you need to save it fast.

Examples:

- "Call Sam."
- "Fix the onboarding copy."
- "Ask Morgan for the deck."
- "Review tomorrow's first meeting."

Quick Capture is not for perfect writing.

It is for getting the thought out of your head.

## 11. Trusted Context

Trusted Context is information the app should rely on.

Use it for facts that should guide planning.

Examples:

- "My best deep work happens before noon."
- "Friday afternoons should stay light."
- "Client X needs same-day follow-up."
- "I prefer direct feedback."
- "Do not schedule optional calls before 10 AM."

Trusted Context should be useful and true.

Do not fill it with random memories.

### Identity Statement

Your identity statement tells Pillar Time who it is helping.

It also tells the app how to speak to you.

It can include:

- your name;
- what you are building;
- what matters most;
- what you tend to avoid;
- what kind of tone helps;
- what you want protected;
- what you do not want the app to do.

Example:

> Help me spend time on work that compounds. Be direct. Do not let urgent noise crowd out the important thing. If my plan does not match my priorities, tell me.

This should make the brief feel like a coaching session, not a formal report.

### Standing Commitments

Standing commitments can also live in Trusted Context.

That helps Pillar Time remember your bigger promises.

Examples:

- "Protect mornings for building when possible."
- "Do not skip weekly review."
- "Follow up with important people within 24 hours."
- "Keep evenings clear unless truly necessary."

### Files And Pasted Context

Pillar Time should let you add context by typing, pasting, or uploading a text file.

This is useful for:

- personal operating notes;
- project plans;
- meeting notes;
- task lists;
- standing commitments;
- identity statements;
- strategy docs.

When you add a file, the app should use it to improve planning.

It should not make you retype everything by hand.

## 12. Meetings

Meetings is where you capture meeting context.

Use it for:

- meeting purpose;
- prep notes;
- decisions;
- follow-ups;
- commitments;
- open questions.

The goal is not perfect meeting notes.

The goal is to keep useful facts from disappearing.

Before a meeting, Pillar Time should help you prepare.

After a meeting, Pillar Time should help turn notes into:

- follow-up tasks;
- future prep;
- commitments;
- reminders;
- Linear work, if needed.

Meeting notes reduce clutter because the next step is not trapped in your memory.

## 13. Reminders And Reviews

### Reminders

Reminders are for future nudges.

They are off by default so the app does not become noisy.

Good reminders are specific.

Examples:

- "Start end-of-day review at 4:45 PM."
- "Check whether the client follow-up was sent."
- "Look at tomorrow's first meeting before stopping work."

Bad reminders are vague.

Examples:

- "Be productive."
- "Do stuff."
- "Remember everything."

If a reminder is not helping, turn it off.

### Reviews

Reviews are recurring planning rituals.

Pillar Time may include templates for:

- morning review;
- midday check;
- end-of-day wrap;
- weekly kickoff;
- monthly review;
- quarterly review;
- annual planning.

Reviews reduce mental clutter because repeated questions get a home.

Instead of worrying all day, you can trust the review to ask the right questions at the right time.

## 14. Intelligence And Sources

Intelligence is the news and source-grounded brief area.

It can use sources such as:

- RSS feeds;
- web pages;
- Reddit;
- X;
- YouTube;
- podcasts;
- documents.

Use Intelligence when you want a report about the outside world.

But Intelligence should not take over the daily planning flow.

The normal day plan should focus on your schedule, commitments, Linear work, reminders, meetings, and trusted context.

News is optional context.

It is not the center of Pillar Time.

### Sources

Sources are places Pillar Time can fetch outside information from.

Examples:

- RSS feeds;
- Reddit threads;
- X searches;
- YouTube channels;
- podcast feeds;
- web pages.

You can turn sources on or off.

If a source fails, that does not always mean something is broken.

It may mean:

- the link is dead;
- the site blocks automated access;
- the service needs login;
- the app is being rate-limited;
- the connector needs a better API setup.

Do not keep 100 sources on just because they exist.

Healthy coverage is better than noisy coverage.

### View Brief From Today

The Today page can open the latest generated brief.

That lets you move from the short day plan to the longer explanation.

The important rule is:

**Today should use the latest day-plan run, not an old cached news run.**

## 15. Approvals

Approvals are one of the most important safety features.

Pillar Time may propose actions.

You decide whether they happen.

Actions that should need approval include:

- create Google Calendar blocks;
- update Google Calendar blocks;
- create Linear issues;
- update Linear issues;
- add Linear comments.

An approval should show what will happen before it happens.

The rule is:

**Pillar Time can suggest. You approve.**

If the approval is old, unclear, or wrong, do not approve it.

Regenerate the plan or make the change manually.

### Telegram Approvals

If Telegram commands are enabled, the app may let you approve from Telegram.

Example:

> /approve calendar

If there is only one clear pending calendar proposal, the app can approve it.

If there is more than one, it should ask you to choose.

It should not guess.

## 16. Settings And Connectors

Settings is where you connect services and check app health.

Common settings include:

- model provider;
- Google Calendar;
- Linear;
- Telegram;
- Reddit;
- X;
- ElevenLabs audio;
- speech-to-text;
- updates;
- connector health.

If something does not work, check Settings first.

### Google Calendar

Google Calendar lets Pillar Time read your schedule.

With write permission, it can also create approved calendar blocks.

Pillar Time should understand the difference between:

- your primary calendar;
- a shared calendar;
- a subscribed calendar;
- an all-day event;
- a timed meeting;
- an event you declined;
- an event you accepted;
- an event you organized;
- an event you are only watching.

This is one of the most important parts of the app.

The app should not treat every calendar equally.

### Linear

Linear lets Pillar Time read and update project work.

You need a Linear personal API key.

The key is saved locally.

The app should not show the saved key back to the screen.

### Model Provider

A model provider helps Pillar Time write better coaching and make better judgments.

Without it, the app should still make a basic plan.

With it, the app should be more useful and more personal.

### Telegram

Telegram can deliver briefs and messages outside the app.

It may also support commands, such as approving a pending calendar proposal.

If Telegram says delivery is unclear, check Telegram before sending again.

The message may have already sent.

### ElevenLabs Audio

ElevenLabs can read briefs aloud.

This is optional.

You do not need it for the main planning features.

### Speech To Text

Speech to text lets you add context by voice.

If local speech-to-text is not installed, Pillar Time may ask if you want to download it.

Voice input should be a convenience, not a requirement.

## 17. Voice, Audio, And Telegram

Voice and messaging should reduce friction.

They should not become more clutter.

Use voice when typing would slow you down.

Use Telegram when you want the plan delivered outside the app.

Use audio when listening is easier than reading.

These features are optional.

The core loop is still:

1. capture context;
2. generate the plan;
3. review the plan;
4. approve actions;
5. do the work.

## 18. Troubleshooting

### I Feel Overwhelmed

Do this:

1. Open Today.
2. Put every loose thought into Quick Capture or Running To-Do.
3. Generate the day plan.
4. Look only at Today's Three.
5. Pick the first honest next step.

Do not try to organize everything first.

Get the pressure out of your head.

### The Day Plan Looks Like A News Report

Use Today and Generate Day Plan.

The normal day plan should focus on Calendar, Linear, tasks, reminders, meetings, commitments, and trusted context.

Intelligence is separate.

### The App Ignores Linear Work

Check Settings and make sure Linear is connected.

Then open Linear and refresh.

If Linear still does not appear in the day plan, regenerate the plan after refresh.

### Calendar Blocks Do Not Get Created

Calendar may be connected for read-only access.

Open Settings and reconnect Google Calendar with write permission.

Then try approving the calendar again.

### The App Thinks An All-Day Event Blocks My Day

Add context and regenerate.

Example:

> This all-day event is shared calendar context. It is not my commitment and should not block scheduling.

### The App Treats Someone Else's Calendar As Mine

Add context and regenerate.

Example:

> Only my primary calendars should be treated as mine. Shared calendars are context unless I accepted the event.

### Telegram Says Delivery Is Unclear

Check Telegram before sending again.

Sometimes the message sends but the app does not get a clear answer quickly.

### The Model Connector Is Not Ready

The app should still make a basic plan.

For better coaching language, set up a model provider in Settings.

### There Are Too Many Sources

Turn off optional sources you do not need.

Pillar Time should work with local context, Calendar, and Linear.

You do not need 100 sources for daily planning.

### The App Feels Noisy

Turn off extra reminders, sources, and connectors.

Then use:

- Today;
- Calendar;
- Linear;
- Planner;
- Trusted Context.

Start simple.

### The Proposed Plan Feels Wrong

Do not approve it.

Add the missing context and regenerate.

Good correction examples:

- "This meeting is more important than it looks."
- "This all-day event is not mine."
- "I need a recovery block after the noon meeting."
- "This task can wait until tomorrow."
- "The real priority is the release, not inbox cleanup."

The goal is not for the first plan to be perfect.

The goal is for the system to learn the important facts quickly.

## 19. Privacy And Safety

Pillar Time is a local desktop app.

It stores local data in SQLite on your machine.

External services are only used when you connect them.

Examples:

- Google Calendar is used if you connect Calendar.
- Linear is used if you connect Linear.
- Telegram is used if you connect Telegram.
- A model provider is used if you configure one.

Do not paste secrets into normal notes.

Put API keys only into the correct connector settings.

Pillar Time should ask before it writes to outside systems.

Pillar Time should not:

- pretend everything is fine when the plan is overloaded;
- treat every calendar as equally important;
- block your whole day because of a normal all-day event;
- write to Calendar or Linear without approval;
- hide connector failures;
- make news the default focus of Today;
- replace your judgment;
- make legal, medical, financial, or emergency decisions for you.

## 20. What Good Use Feels Like

Pillar Time is working well when:

- you know the few things that matter today;
- your calendar has protected blocks for real work;
- all-day shared events are not treated as hard blockers;
- Linear work appears when it matters;
- reminders are helpful, not noisy;
- the app asks before writing to outside systems;
- missing context is easy to add;
- you feel less need to hold the whole day in your head.

The best version of Pillar Time should feel like this:

> I can see the day clearly. I know what matters. I know what can wait. I know what needs approval. I can stop carrying the whole plan in my head.

That is the whole point.
